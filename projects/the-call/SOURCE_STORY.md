# The Call

**3-minute film about a cinematographer winning a prestigious film contest**

## Setup (0:00 - 0:45)

Late evening in a small apartment. MAYA, a 28-year-old cinematographer with worn film school loans and dreams bigger than her day job, sits at her laptop refreshing the festival website obsessively. Her partner JAMES (32, supportive but pragmatic) brings coffee. Their rescue dog SCOUT (golden retriever mix, loyal companion) rests her head on Maya's lap.

The email arrives. Maya's hands shake. She can't open it.

## Climax (0:45 - 2:15)

James reads it aloud: "We are thrilled to inform you... Best Cinematography... International Film Festival."

Maya freezes. James whoops. Scout jumps up, sensing the energy, tail wagging wildly.

Cut to: A montage of their reactions. Phone calls to Maya's mother CAROL (60s, proud, tears streaming). To her mentor DAVID (55), the veteran DP who believed in her when she was shooting wedding videos.

Back in the apartment: Maya watches the festival video of her film playing on the big screen. Her shots—the ones she fought for, the ones shot in 16mm on a shoestring budget, the ones that kept her awake at night worrying about focus—are enormous, beautiful, and being applauded by hundreds of filmmakers.

## Resolution (2:15 - 3:00)

Three months later: Maya sits in the director's chair on a real film set. A crew around her. Her name on the call sheet. Scout is there too—the production's unofficial mascot, trotting between monitors.

Final shot: Maya looking through the camera, that familiar gesture but now with the weight of validation. Scout's silhouette in the foreground. The camera pushes in on Maya's eye as she calls out: "Roll sound. Roll camera. And... action."

Fade to black. Titles roll over the sound of a film set in motion.

**Tone:** Intimate, hopeful, rooted in the real struggle and joy of a working artist. Bittersweet but ultimately affirming.
